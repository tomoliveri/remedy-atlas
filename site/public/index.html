Research roadmap

From the first targets to cancer

Follow the milestones from open research to better care. We check off evidence reviews as they finish and report patient outcomes when they are measured. Each cancer type has its own path.

  1. Underway

    Work through the first disease targets

    Start with drug-resistant infections and snakebite. Keep the strongest next questions in the queue as reviews finish.

    • In parallel

      Begin with cervical cancer

      Our first cancer question is where people are lost between HPV screening, follow-up and treatment. Pick one setting where the evidence and an implementation partner could make a decision actionable.

        WHO cervical cancer evidence

      • As the network grows

        Build separate programs for other cancers

        Study access to molecular testing and matched care for lung cancer, and whether earlier detection leads to timely care for people at high risk of liver cancer. Add further programs based on addressable mortality, expertise and a credible route to better outcomes.

        Review candidate cancer programs

      • Once the evidence supports a test

        Validate with independent partners

        For a proven screening or treatment program, the next step may be an implementation study with a health service. A new drug, biomarker or diagnostic may need wet-lab validation before clinical studies. A qualified partner scopes the work, approvals, budget and criteria for moving forward.

        How laboratory work would be funded

      • After validation

        Demonstrate patient benefit

        Clinical partners compare the intervention with current care and measure outcomes that matter to patients. For a new treatment, this includes appropriate clinical trials and regulatory review before routine use.

        How cancer clinical trials work

      • After clinical benefit is established

        Deliver care and measure lives saved

        Work with health systems on affordability, access and delivery. Track who receives care, its harms, and whether deaths fall. Report the measured change in each population and use what we learn to choose the next research question.

      Independent funding

      Donate to a defined validation study

      No campaign is open. If an independent partner confirms wet-lab work is needed, we’ll publish the study scope and budget before asking for donations.

      Donors would give through the partner foundation’s official checkout to a restricted study fund. The foundation would hold the funds, pay approved study costs and report what was spent. Remedy Atlas maintainers would have no financial access or payment authority.

      Lives-saved ranges illustrate what could happen if a future treatment succeeds. The baseline counts come from published sources. Eligibility, reach and treatment effects are assumptions.

      From research to outcomes

      How we estimate lives saved

      We start with reported deaths or clinical detections in a defined population. Each card shows the assumed eligible share, treatment reach, and additional mortality reduction compared with current care.

      An improvement can save lives only if it works in clinical care and reaches patients in time. Actual benefit may be zero. Overlapping tracks can cover the same patients, so their ranges cannot be added.
